## FCC records

### FCC ownership reports

Every two years, each commercial TV station's licensee files an ownership report with the FCC (Form 323, required by [47 CFR 73.3615](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-73.3615)). It names the licensee and the companies and people that own it, up through parent companies, and lists contracts that bear on control of the station.

These reports are **the record** on the project page. Stations are grouped by the owners the reports disclose, with parent companies rolled up, and the market is scored on that grouping. The Baltimore history in section 2 uses the reports from every two-year cycle from 2009 to 2023, including the older ones the FCC carried over from its previous database.

You can read any station's reports under "Ownership Reports" in its [public file](#station-public-files), or get all of them in bulk from the [LMS database files](#fcc-licensing-and-management-system).

### FCC Licensing and Management System

The FCC's licensing database for broadcast stations. The FCC publishes its public tables as a single download that it replaces every day. The study uses the download of October 1, 2026, and takes from it:

- each station's licensee of record, service and status, which decide the 1,347 stations counted
- the ownership reports above, with the contracts listed in them
- each station's Nielsen market, and each market's TV households (the FCC's table carries 2015 figures)
- sales that were filed but not yet approved. The database lists the buyer as licensee as soon as a sale is filed, so for the 12 stations with one pending, the study uses the licensee in the station's latest ownership report.

Because the download is replaced daily, the October 1 files can't be downloaded from the FCC again. The analysis keeps a copy, with checksums.

**Link:** [LMS Public Database Files](https://enterpriseefiling.fcc.gov/dataentry/public/tv/lmsDatabase.html) (FCC)

### Agreements listed in ownership reports

Ownership reports have to list contracts that bear on control, and the FCC's database holds thousands of LMA, JSA and SSA entries. Each entry is attached to a filing, which can cover many stations, and has no field for the station being run or which side runs it; some entries put a call sign in their title, most don't. Section 5 has the numbers. So these entries can't reliably say on their own which station is run by whom.

The study uses them two ways: as the source for the four stations run by private regional companies (Morgan Murphy Media's KVCT and KFJX, Bahakel's WNCF and Coastal's WMDN), and as a check on the biggest sidecar companies, Cunningham, Deerfield and Mission, which they name hundreds of times. They also list Vaughan's agreements, under its stations' names. They never cover American Spirit's six stations. They're in the `ownership_reports_contracts` table of the [LMS database files](#fcc-licensing-and-management-system).

### Station public files

The FCC hosts an online public file for every station. Stations have to post copies of their LMAs, JSAs and SSAs there ([47 CFR 73.3526](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-73.3526), paragraphs (e)(14), (16) and (18)), along with their ownership reports and other filings. The files can be searched one station at a time.

The study uses one station's file directly. KAUZ's file holds its 2009 shared services and advertising agreements with KSWO's owner, a 2014 amendment, and its 2015 services agreement with Raycom, amended in 2018. Those documents date the Wichita Falls arrangement described in section 2.

**Links:** [search the public files](https://publicfiles.fcc.gov/) · [KAUZ-TV's file](https://publicfiles.fcc.gov/tv-profile/kauz-tv) (FCC)

## Operators' SEC filings

Public companies file annual reports (Form 10-K) and reports of major events (Form 8-K) with the Securities and Exchange Commission, and they're legally liable for what's in them. The station groups list the stations they run under agreements with other licensees in these filings. That's why the study's list of agreements is built from them: the agreements for 59 of the 63 stations are cited station by station to one of the three filings below. The other four come from the [agreements listed in ownership reports](#agreements-listed-in-ownership-reports).

### Nexstar's 2025 annual report

Form 10-K for 2025, filed February 27, 2026. Its station table (Item 1) and Note 2 on variable interest entities list the stations Nexstar runs under agreements with other licensees, such as Mission Broadcasting and White Knight Broadcasting. The source for Nexstar's 37 stations.

**Link:** [Nexstar Media Group, Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1142417/000119312526078361/nxst-20251231.htm) (SEC EDGAR)

### Sinclair's 2025 annual report

Form 10-K for 2025, filed February 27, 2026. Its station list ("Television Markets and Stations," pages 9–10) marks the stations Sinclair runs under agreements with sidecar companies such as Cunningham Broadcasting and Deerfield Media, and a related-party note (page F-41) covers Cunningham. It's also the source for Baltimore: the WUTB agreement and Sinclair's purchase of WUTB in October 2025. The source for Sinclair's 16 stations.

**Link:** [Sinclair, Inc., Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1971213/000197121326000012/sbgi-20251231.htm) (SEC EDGAR)

### Gray's July 2026 purchase announcement

Form 8-K filed July 1, 2026, Exhibit 99.2: Gray's announcement that it would buy American Spirit Media's six stations for $50 million. The first closing, on July 1, started what the filing calls a "limited local management agreement" under which Gray runs the six stations until the purchase is complete; the filing doesn't describe its terms. It also says Gray has provided back-office services to five of them for more than a decade. The source for Gray's six stations.

**Link:** [Gray Media, Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/43196/000143774926022218/ex_982906.htm) (SEC EDGAR)

## Rules, orders and cases

### The local ownership rule

[47 CFR 73.3555](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-73.3555) (eCFR). Paragraph (b) sets how many TV stations one company may "own, operate, or control" in a market: two, since the court decision below struck down its ban on combining two top-four stations. Note 2 sets which ties count toward that limit: (j) makes an LMA count once it covers more than 15% of a station's weekly airtime, and (k) covers joint sales agreements.

### Zimmer Radio v. FCC (2025)

[The Eighth Circuit's opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/25/07/241380P.pdf), *Zimmer Radio of Mid-Missouri, Inc. v. FCC*, 145 F.4th 828 (8th Cir. July 23, 2025). It vacated the local rule's ban on combining two of a market's four highest-rated stations and upheld the two-station limit. Its mandate issued October 23, 2025.

### The FCC's 2014 joint sales rule

Announced March 31, 2014, in a [news release](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-326350A1.pdf), and adopted in [order FCC 14-28](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-14-28A1.pdf). It made TV joint sales agreements count toward the ownership limits. The page's quotes "a loophole," "consolidation in excess of what is permitted" and "competition, localism, and diversity in local broadcast markets" are from the news release.

### The FCC's 2016 order

[Order FCC 16-107](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-16-107A1.pdf), released August 25, 2016. It re-adopted the joint sales rule after a federal appeals court vacated it on procedural grounds, and it required stations to put shared services agreements in their public files. The quote "a necessary step" is from paragraph 377.

### The FCC's 2017 repeal

[Order FCC 17-156](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-17-156A1.pdf), adopted November 16, 2017, and in effect from February 7, 2018. It ended the rule counting TV joint sales agreements toward the limits.

### FCC v. Prometheus Radio Project (2021)

[The Supreme Court's opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-1231_i425.pdf), 592 U.S. 414. The Court unanimously upheld the FCC's 2017 order, reversing a 2019 Third Circuit decision that had vacated it, and held that the agency didn't have to commission new studies before loosening its ownership rules.

### The 2023 Merger Guidelines

[The Justice Department and Federal Trade Commission's guidelines](https://www.justice.gov/atr/2023-merger-guidelines) for reviewing mergers. They call a market with an HHI above 1,800 highly concentrated, and presume that a merger raising HHI by more than 100 points in such a market harms competition.

### The Nexstar-TEGNA injunction (2026)

Nexstar [announced that it had closed its purchase of TEGNA](https://www.nexstar.tv/nexstar-media-group-inc-closes-acquisition-of-tegna-inc/) on March 19, 2026. On April 17, 2026, in a suit by eight state attorneys general and DIRECTV, the federal court for the Eastern District of California [ordered the companies to halt integration and keep TEGNA separate](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/court-filings/nexstar-tegna-merger-litigation-preliminary-injunction-2026.pdf) while the case proceeds. That's why the study counts TEGNA's stations as TEGNA's.
